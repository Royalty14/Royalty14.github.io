How to Spot and Avoid Job Scams in South Africa

Last updated 8 October 2026

Fake job adverts are common in South Africa, and they target people who are desperate for work. Knowing the warning signs protects your money and your personal information.

The number one rule

Never pay to apply for a job, learnership or internship. Real employers and government departments don't charge application, registration, uniform, training or "processing" fees.

Warning signs

  • You're asked to pay anything, or to buy airtime, a starter kit or a course before you're hired.
  • The advert promises a high salary for very little work or no qualifications.
  • Contact is only by WhatsApp or a personal Gmail address instead of the company's official domain.
  • You're "hired" without an interview, or the interview happens only by chat.
  • They ask for your ID number, banking details or a copy of your bank card early on.
  • Pressure to act fast: "only 3 spots left, pay today".
  • Poor spelling and a vague company name with no website or physical address.

How to check an advert

  • Find the job on the company's own careers website. Big employers like Shoprite, Transnet, banks and Eskom post every vacancy there.
  • For government posts, check the DPSA vacancy circular on dpsa.gov.za or the department's own website.
  • Search the company name plus "scam" to see if others have reported it.
  • Phone the company on a number from its official website, not the one in the message.

That's why every listing on Connections Jobs SA links to the official source, so you can see the real advert yourself.

If you've been targeted

Stop contact and don't send money. If you've paid or shared banking details, contact your bank immediately. Report fraud to the South African Police Service, and report fake adverts to the platform where you saw them.
